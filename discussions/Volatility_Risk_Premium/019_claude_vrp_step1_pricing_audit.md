## 2. What the notebook tests

| Part | Test | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| A | Reprice each near-ATM bid and ask from its own exchange IV, using our $T$ and units | median \|error\| ≤ 0.25 half-spread |
| B | Exchange IV time basis: fit $\log(\sigma_{ours}/\sigma_{ex})=c+\tfrac12\delta/T$ on bid and ask separately | \|δ\| ≤ 2 min |
| C | `option_iv_atm_forward` vs the IV that reprices the ATM straddle mid | median \|log ratio\| ≤ 2%, no TTE bucket beyond ±3% |
| D | Terminal / 30m sign flip, moving one ingredient at a time (clock allocation, settlement weighting, horizon), on own rows and common rows | every flip attributed |
| E | 5-minute index RV vs RV from 30-minute returns per session | median ratio in [0.8, 1.25] |

Part A reprices bid and ask from their own IVs because averaging bid and ask IVs before pricing adds a convexity bias. The first version did that, and on synthetic data it produced a spurious 0.3–0.7-minute offset.

## 3. Synthetic validation

- **With a true offset of 0** (quotes generated by Black-76 from a known IV): every gate passes, repricing error is 0.0 half-spreads and δ = 0.0.
- **With the exchange IV computed on $T+3$ minutes:** B estimates δ = 2.92–2.93 minutes for every asset and fails as intended. A still passes (0.06 half-spreads), which is why B exists.
